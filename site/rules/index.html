Where you can and cannot dump RV waste in Florida

In short. Florida law limits where a recreational vehicle may empty black water, grey water, or any other liquid waste. This is an unofficial reading of the public texts, not legal advice.

Florida law limits where a recreational vehicle may empty black water, grey water, or any other liquid waste. This page quotes the statute, the Department of Health park rule, the Florida Litter Law, the sanitary-nuisance statute, the pollution statute, and the FDOT rest-area rule. Emptying an RV tank

Section 513.08(1), Florida Statutes, says: "It is unlawful to empty any receptacle or fixture containing human excreta, human urine, or any other liquid waste from a mobile home or recreational vehicle or at a recreational campsite except into a sewerage system approved by the department."

The prohibition is not limited to the toilet tank. It covers any other liquid waste from the recreational vehicle, which includes what travelers usually call grey water.

Section 513.08(2) says the operator or permittee of a mobile home park, lodging park, recreational vehicle park, or recreational camp shall provide the means for emptying those receptacles, and for cleaning them, as specified in the rules of the department.

Section 513.01(1) defines "Department" for this chapter as the Department of Health, including its representative county health departments. [source, source]

What a lawful park dump station is

Rule 64E-15.004, Florida Administrative Code (Sewage Disposal), is the Department of Health rule for sewage at mobile home parks, lodging parks, recreational vehicle parks, and recreational camps. The Florida Administrative Code record lists that rule as last amended effective 30 December 2009. The wording below is from the Department of Health copy of Chapter 64E-15 posted by the Glades County health department. Its history note matches the Florida Administrative Code record. [source, source]

Subsection (1) requires a safe method of sewage collection, disposal, or treatment and disposal at each park or camp, in compliance with either Chapter 64E-6, F.A.C., Standards for Onsite Sewage Treatment and Disposal Systems, or Chapter 62-600, F.A.C., Domestic Wastewater Facilities. Chapter 62-600 is a Department of Environmental Protection chapter. The Florida Administrative Code entry for Rule 64E-6.001 is titled "General (Transferred)", and its history note says "Transferred to 62-6.001." Chapter 62-6 is the Department of Environmental Protection chapter with the same title, Standards for Onsite Sewage Treatment and Disposal Systems. Rule 64E-15.004, as posted in the Department of Health copy, still names Chapter 64E-6. [source, source, source, source]

Rule 64E-15.001(10) defines "Sanitary Dump Station" as "a properly designed facility used for receiving and disposing of liquid wastes from recreational vehicle holding tanks." That definitions rule is listed as effective 20 May 1996. Subsection (11) defines a self-contained unit as a recreational vehicle which has a flush toilet, bathtub or shower, handwashing compartment, and internal storage compartments of potable water supply and sewage holding. [source, source]

Rule 64E-15.004(5) says: "All recreational vehicle parks shall be provided with at least one easily accessible sanitary dump station for the use of occupants or patrons." Parks constructed after the effective date of the rule must provide a sanitary dump station for each 100 spaces, or fraction thereof, or for each 250 spaces, or fraction thereof, when more than fifty percent of the spaces have sewage disposal hookups.

Subsection (6) says a sanitary dump station shall consist of a trapped four inch sewer riser pipe connected to an approved sewage system, surrounded at the inlet end by a concrete apron sloped to the drain, with a suitably sized and hinged cover. A wash-down water outlet must be protected from backflow and back-siphonage.

Subsection (7) requires a sign immediately adjacent to the wash-down hose, in letters of at least three inches height, reading: WATER NOT SAFE FOR DRINKING.

Subsection (4) says the connection point for all sewer outlets shall be tightly capped when not in use.

There is a written exemption. Rule 64E-15.005(6), listed as amended effective 7 June 2007, says that if a park owner files a letter with the county health department stating the park has a potable water and sewer hook-up at each site and the park only rents to self-contained recreational vehicles, the park is exempt from the dump-station requirements in subsections 64E-15.004(5), (6), and (7). [source, source]

Ground, ditches, storm drains, and open water

Section 403.413 is the Florida Litter Law. Subsection (5) says that unless otherwise authorized by law or permit, it is unlawful for any person to dump raw human waste from any train, aircraft, motor vehicle, or vessel upon the public or private lands or waters of the state. Subsection (2) defines "dump" to include drain, discharge, or dispose of. The same subsection defines "motor vehicle" to include an automobile, truck, trailer, semitrailer, and any other vehicle that is powered by a motor. [source]

Section 403.413(6)(j) says any person who violates subsection (5) is guilty of a misdemeanor of the second degree, punishable as provided in section 775.082 or section 775.083. Section 775.082(4)(b) provides a definite term of imprisonment not exceeding 60 days for a second-degree misdemeanor. Section 775.083(1)(e) says that fine shall not exceed $500. The same litter-law paragraph says that any person who dumps more than 500 pounds or more than 100 cubic feet of raw human waste, or who dumps any quantity of such waste for commercial purposes, is guilty of a felony of the third degree. Section 775.082(3)(e) provides a term of imprisonment not exceeding 5 years for a third-degree felony, and section 775.083(1)(c) says that fine shall not exceed $5,000. Paragraphs (1)(f) and (1)(g) of section 775.083 allow a higher amount equal to double the gain or the loss, or a higher amount a statute specifically authorizes. Subsection (5) uses the words "raw human waste." It does not use the words "grey water." [source, source, source]

Section 386.041(1)(a) says untreated or improperly treated human waste, when existing, permitted, maintained, kept, or caused by any individual, municipal organization, or corporation, governmental or private, shall constitute prima facie evidence of maintaining a nuisance injurious to health. Section 386.01 defines a sanitary nuisance as the commission of any act, or the keeping, maintaining, propagation, existence, or permission of anything, by which the health or life of a person may be threatened or impaired, or by which or through which, directly or indirectly, disease may be caused. Section 386.051 says any person found guilty of creating, keeping, or maintaining a nuisance injurious to health shall be guilty of a misdemeanor of the second degree, punishable as provided in section 775.082 or section 775.083. [source, source, source]

Section 403.161(1)(a) is the general pollution prohibition. It is not written only for recreational vehicles. It prohibits any person from causing pollution, except as otherwise provided in that chapter, so as to harm or injure human health or welfare, animal, plant, or aquatic life, or property. Subsection (3) says a person who willfully commits that violation commits a felony of the third degree, by a fine of not more than $50,000 or by imprisonment for 5 years, or both, for each offense. Subsection (4) says a violation of paragraph (1)(a) from reckless indifference or gross careless disregard is a misdemeanor of the second degree, by a fine of not more than $10,000 or by 60 days in jail, or both, for each offense. [source]

None of these sections names a storm drain, a roadside ditch, or bare ground as an approved place to empty an RV tank.

FDOT rest areas, welcome centers, and service plazas

FDOT Rule 14-28.002, F.A.C., effective 5 December 2018, is titled Public Use of Rest Areas and Welcome Centers, Truck Comfort Stations, and Wayside Parks. The rule text posted by FDOT does not mention a sanitary dump station. It prohibits camping. Parking at rest areas and welcome centers is limited to three hours, except for commercial motor vehicle operators and persons permitted under Rule 14-28.005. For commercial motor vehicle operators subject to hours-of-service regulations, parking is limited to ten hours. The rule says no person shall hook up a vehicle to electrical and water outlets. Bottles, broken glass, ashes, waste paper, or other rubbish shall be left only at places provided for disposal. [source, source]

On 26 October 2026 the Florida Department of Transportation rest area information page did not mention dump stations. It repeats the three-hour stay and the ban on overnight camping. The public rest-facility list is built from the Florida Department of Transportation map service fdotRestFacilities, layer fdotAMFacilities. That layer's amenity fields are Full Facility, Nighttime Security, Family Restroom, Adult Changing Table, Fenced Dog Area, Sun Pass Kiosk, Vending Machines, and ATM (automated teller machine). There is no dump-station field. The facility-list page filter offers Full Facilities, Nighttime Security, Family Restrooms, Adult Changing Tables, Dog Park Areas, Sun Pass Kiosks, Vending Machines, and ATMs (automated teller machines). It does not offer a dump station. [source, source, source]

When no dump station is close

  1. Keep black water and grey water in the holding tanks until you reach a sewerage system approved by the Department of Health. Section 513.08 does not allow any other emptying. Section 403.413(5) separately prohibits dumping raw human waste from a motor vehicle onto the lands or waters of the state unless authorized by law or permit.
  2. If you are a guest at a permitted recreational vehicle park, ask the office where occupants empty tanks. Section 513.08(2) puts that duty on the operator. If the site has a sewer connection, Rule 64E-15.004(3) says the connection shall be self draining and leak proof from liquids and gases and be connected by means of a readily removable, acid resistant material. Subsection (4) says the connection point for all sewer outlets shall be tightly capped when not in use. A park can be exempt from a separate dump station only when the owner has filed the full-hookup letter described in Rule 64E-15.005(6).
  3. Inside a park or camp, Rule 64E-15.004(1) allows a transportable wastewater container or a cassette-type toilet only on the conditions in that subsection. The container must be specifically designed and sold for holding and transporting wastewater, with a tight-capping lid and watertight connections that under normal use will not leak or spill. Waste from hand washing sinks, kitchen sinks, and showers may go into a transportable wastewater container. Toilet waste may not. A cassette-type portable toilet may hold toilet waste if the storage container can be sealed so it will not leak or spill during transportation. Both must be transported to an approved disposal site without leakage, spillage, or a sanitary nuisance. If waste spills on the ground, the person transporting the tank shall immediately notify park management. The rule makes park managers responsible for enforcement and requires a disinfection protocol on file. It does not name a roadside, a rest area, or a storm drain as an approved disposal site.
  4. Drive to a dump station that discharges to an approved system, and call ahead. Regional lists on this site separate official operator pages from community points. Many Florida State Parks pages describe the station as a campground amenity and do not say that a day visitor may use it. Anastasia State Park says: "A communal dump station is free for park campers." [source] Parks that do state a rule are quoted on the state park dump page.
  5. The statutes and rules quoted on this page do not describe a statewide mobile pump-out for a traveler stopped on the roadside. They also do not authorize emptying a tank on the ground while waiting.
