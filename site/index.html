Florida RV dump stations: sourced list by region

A plain list of places in Florida where an RV can empty black and grey tanks, taken only from operator pages (Florida State Parks, Love's) and OpenStreetMap community data. 43 stations on 26 October 2026: 11 confirmed on an official page, 32 community-mapped.

Call ahead. Hours, fees, gate codes and who may use a station change often, and many park stations are for registered campers only. Confirm with the operator before you drive there.

How to use a dump station

  1. Pull up so your sewer outlet is on the drain side. Set the parking brake.
  2. Wear disposable gloves. Connect the sewer hose to your outlet first, then put the other end fully into the drain inlet and secure it.
  3. Open the black tank valve first and let it empty completely.
  4. Close it, then open the grey tank valve. Grey water helps flush the hose.
  5. Close the valves, rinse the hose with the non-potable rinse water if provided, and drain it into the inlet.
  6. Disconnect at the RV end first, rinse the area, and close the drain lid.

General practice guidance, not from a single official source. Always follow posted signs at the station.

Etiquette

  • Be quick when others are waiting. Do tank flushing and hose cleaning at your site if you can.
  • Never use the rinse hose to fill your fresh water tank.
  • Leave the pad cleaner than you found it.
  • Pay any posted fee and respect campers-only signs.
  • Do not dump grey or black water anywhere other than an approved inlet. It is a health hazard.
Verification levels
  • Official the operator's own page (Florida State Parks, Love's) states a dump station, quoted in the table.
  • Official + OSM official page plus an OpenStreetMap point that names the same operator, used only for coordinates and extra tags.
  • Community (OpenStreetMap) mapped by volunteers as amenity=sanitary_dump_station [source]. Name, fee and access tags are often missing. Treat as a lead, not a confirmation.

Short FAQ

Can I dump at a Florida interstate rest area?

Not according to any FDOT page we checked. The FDOT rest area page and facility list do not mention dump stations, and FDOT says overnight camping is not permitted and visitors may stay up to three hours [source, source]. Some third-party apps list rest area dumps; we could not confirm those on an official source. The statute, the health rule, and the FDOT rest-area rule are on the dumping rules page.

Can I use a state park dump station if I am not camping there?

Usually not stated. Anastasia State Park says its communal dump station is free for park campers [source]. Other park pages we read do not say whether day visitors may dump. Ask the ranger station first.

Do I need a gate code?

At some parks, yes. Rainbow Springs says its campground gates are closed at all times and the code is given on your day of arrival [source].

Are OpenStreetMap entries reliable?

They are community data [source]. Many Florida entries have no name, fee or access tags. We show them so you know where to look, flagged as Community, and they need a phone call to confirm.

Is there potable water at the dump?

Rarely stated by official sources. Where an OSM contributor tagged water_point we show it as an OSM tag. Never use the rinse hose for drinking water.
